The pitch was simple: merchants take a 30-second video of a product, the platform returns a shoppable 3D viewer and a Shopify embed. Behind that sentence is a pipeline I've been hardening for the better part of a year.

## The pipeline

```
Capture (phone video)
   ↓
COLMAP — Structure-from-Motion (camera poses + sparse cloud)
   ↓
Nerfstudio (splatfacto) — Gaussian Splatting training on the GPU
   ↓
SparkJS — per-splat SDF color editing in the browser
   ↓
Isolation — remove background, lock to the product
   ↓
Export — bundle for Shopify (3D viewer + GLB + AR-ready file)
```

Each stage has a failure mode that took me longer than I want to admit to characterize:

- **COLMAP**: silently fails on featureless products. White bottles are the worst. The fix is a controlled capture rig with a textured turntable; the fallback is a quality gate that tells the merchant "your capture isn't dense enough — try again".
- **Nerfstudio**: GPU memory is the wall. A 4K input video at default settings OOMs a 24GB card. Aggressive downsampling + variant of `splatfacto-big` was the right tradeoff for production.
- **SparkJS**: brilliant for SDF color editing, but its sparse-cloud quality bar is high. Garbage in, weird color halos out.

## What "production-grade" actually meant

I drafted this as a fast prototype, then spent two months turning it into something I'd let a paying merchant near. The substantive shifts:

1. **From sync to SSE-streamed jobs.** The pipeline is minutes-to-tens-of-minutes. You can't block a browser request on it. FastAPI + Celery + Server-Sent Events gives merchants live progress without polling.
2. **From single-tenant to multi-tenant-aware.** The closed-beta foundation isn't fully multi-tenant yet, but every layer is *aware* of tenancy — per-tenant storage prefixes, per-tenant Stripe metering, per-tenant rate limits. Retrofitting that later would have been miserable.
3. **From "demo embed" to real Shopify integration.** The embed has to survive Shopify's checkout flow, work on mobile, and not blow the page's INP budget. That meant lazy-loading the splat viewer, pre-warming the WASM SparkJS bundle, and serving a lightweight fallback for low-end devices.

## The non-engineering work that mattered most

Two pieces I underestimated:

- **The capture instruction page.** Every merchant who failed at COLMAP did so because they shot a 360° pan in 11 seconds instead of 30. A two-minute instructional video saved more support tickets than any engineering refinement.
- **The pricing model.** The pipeline is GPU-heavy. If you charge per video, you have to know your unit economics cold. I built a per-stage cost telemetry layer before pricing was even on the roadmap — that paid for itself the first time I tried to model gross margin.

## The honest status

The pipeline works end-to-end. The SaaS layer (auth, multi-tenancy, monitoring, billing) is still being hardened. I'd describe the product as "closed-beta foundation" — the moving parts are there, the rough edges are being filed off, the production-gap tracker in the repo is a candid list of what's left.

That candor is by design. The number of "AI 3D for e-commerce" startups that demo well and ship nothing is not small. I'd rather have a smaller surface that actually holds up.
